ApartWorld.

A B2B serviced-apartment booking platform connecting travel agents, agency admins, and operations teams. I joined as the embedded researcher and ran four studies — from understanding the ecosystem to usability-testing the flows that shaped the MVP.

The Context

Not a booking screen — an operational system.

The team had feature requirements and the instinct to start designing screens. I pushed back. Different stakeholders described the same process in completely different ways, and we were entering a category with established players and fixed expectations.

So I started with two questions instead: how does work actually move between the people who'd use this, and what does the category already leave underserved?

The reframe

“Research changed the question from ‘what should the interface look like?’ to ‘how does work actually happen — and where do we focus first?’”

Who ApartWorld serves

Agents

Optimising for speed

Booking fast for a waiting client — location & price first, then certainty.

Admins

Optimising for control

Approvals, KYC, credit limits, performance — across three admin roles.

Ops

Keeping it running

Tickets, modifications, failures — the work that happens after “confirm”.

What the research changed

From “add more features” to a coherent first version.

The work reframed the product strategy toward the underserved B2B agent layer, established what the operational infrastructure had to support from day one, and produced an MVP scope the whole team could line up behind.

  • Strategy: build B2B-agent-first, then expand toward direct consumers.
  • Trust framework: a phased rating strategy instead of borrowing hotel stars.
  • MVP scope: compare, quotation, property highlights & workflow continuity — deferring AI search & loyalty.
